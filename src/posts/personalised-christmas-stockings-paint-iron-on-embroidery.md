Personalised stockings are one of those Christmas traditions that feel special every year. A name on a stocking says "you belong here", which is why they're lovely for new babies, new partners and new pets joining the family.

You don't need a sewing machine or an embroidery business to make one. There are three main ways to add a name to a plain stocking at home: **fabric paint**, **iron-on lettering** and **hand embroidery**. Each has a different look, cost and lifespan. Here's how they compare, with step-by-step instructions for each.

## Start with the right stocking

Your method depends partly on the fabric:

- **Cotton canvas and calico:** the easiest surface for all three methods.
- **Felt:** great for embroidery and glue-on felt letters; iron-on vinyl can work on some felts, but test first because synthetic felt can melt.
- **Knitted stockings:** best for embroidery or a separate name tag; paint and iron-on don't sit well on stretchy knit.
- **Velvet and faux fur cuffs:** hard to personalise directly. Add a name to a separate cuff or a hanging tag instead.

**Tip:** if you can, choose a stocking with a plain, flat cuff. The cuff is the usual spot for the name and it's much easier to work on than the curved foot.

## Method 1: Fabric paint

**Time:** 30 to 60 minutes, plus drying and heat-setting
**Cost:** low
**Look:** hand-lettered, casual, flexible on colour

### You'll need

- Fabric paint or fabric paint pens
- A pencil or water-soluble fabric marker
- Cardboard to slip inside the stocking
- Stencils (optional)
- An iron (for heat-setting, if the paint requires it)

### Steps

1. **Pre-wash** if the fabric is washable, to remove sizing that can stop paint absorbing. Dry and iron flat.
2. **Insert cardboard** inside the stocking so paint doesn't bleed through to the back.
3. **Sketch the name** lightly. Use a printed name as a guide underneath if the fabric is thin enough to see through, or use a stencil.
4. **Paint in thin layers.** A second coat after the first dries gives stronger colour than one heavy coat.
5. **Dry fully.** In humid weather allow extra time.
6. **Heat-set if the product says to.** Usually this means ironing on the reverse side for a few minutes with a cloth between. Follow your paint's instructions exactly.

### Pros and cons

- **Pros:** cheapest option, any colour, easy for kids to help with.
- **Cons:** hand lettering takes practice; paint can crack on very stiff or textured fabrics.

## Method 2: Iron-on lettering (heat transfer vinyl)

**Time:** 30 to 45 minutes
**Cost:** low to medium
**Look:** crisp, clean, store-bought finish

### You'll need

- Heat transfer vinyl (HTV) *or* pre-cut iron-on letters
- A cutting machine for custom names (optional)
- A household iron or heat press
- Baking paper or a pressing cloth
- A firm, heat-safe surface (an ironing board is often too soft; a towel on a bench works better)

### Steps

1. **Cut the name in mirror image** if using HTV with a cutting machine. The shiny carrier side goes face down on the cutting mat.
2. **Weed** away the excess vinyl.
3. **Pre-press** the stocking for a few seconds to remove moisture and wrinkles. In humid weather this step matters more than you'd think.
4. **Position the name** on the cuff and cover with baking paper.
5. **Press firmly** with the iron at the temperature the vinyl instructions specify, holding rather than sliding. Work section by section for longer names.
6. **Peel** the carrier sheet, hot or cold depending on the vinyl type.
7. **Wait** at least 24 hours before washing, and wash inside out on a gentle cycle.

### Pros and cons

- **Pros:** sharp, professional look; glitter and metallic vinyls look great under tree lights.
- **Cons:** can lift or crack if under-pressed; not suitable for knit, fur or some synthetic felts.

## Method 3: Hand embroidery

**Time:** 2 to 4 hours per name, depending on length and stitch
**Cost:** low
**Look:** classic, textured, heirloom

### You'll need

- Embroidery floss (6-stranded cotton)
- An embroidery needle
- An embroidery hoop that fits the cuff
- A water-soluble fabric marker
- Small sharp scissors

### Steps

1. **Write the name** on the cuff with a water-soluble marker. Joined-up script is the most forgiving for beginners because there are fewer starts and stops.
2. **Hoop the fabric** so it's taut but not stretched.
3. **Choose your stitch:**
   - *Backstitch* is the easiest and gives a neat line.
   - *Chain stitch* gives a thicker, cosy look.
   - *Split stitch* is smooth and good for curves.
4. **Use 2 to 3 strands** of floss for fine lettering, 4 to 6 for bolder letters.
5. **Stitch along your lines**, keeping stitches a consistent length.
6. **Finish neatly** on the back by weaving the tail under existing stitches.
7. **Rinse or dab away** the marker with cold water once you're done.

### Pros and cons

- **Pros:** most durable when done well; looks and feels handmade in the best way; a great evening project in front of the cricket.
- **Cons:** slowest option; access to the inside of a sewn stocking can be awkward.

**Shortcut:** if your stocking is already sewn up, stitch the name on a separate strip of felt or calico and sew or glue that onto the cuff.

## Side-by-side comparison

| | Fabric paint | Iron-on (HTV) | Embroidery |
|---|---|---|---|
| Time per name | Short | Short | Long |
| Cost | Low | Low to medium | Low |
| Skill needed | Some lettering practice | Machine or careful placement | Patience more than skill |
| Durability | Good if heat-set | Good if pressed properly | Excellent |
| Works on knit/fur | No | No | Knit yes; fur no |
| Kid-friendly | Yes | Adult does the iron | Older kids |

## Which should you choose?

- **Making several stockings at once?** Iron-on lettering is fastest and most consistent.
- **Want kids involved?** Fabric paint, with an adult on heat-setting duty.
- **Making a stocking for a new baby that you hope lasts decades?** Embroidery is worth the time.

## Don't forget the pets

Pet stockings are a fun extra. Use the same methods, add a paw print, and maybe skip the edible treats inside until Christmas morning. Dogs can find a stocking full of treats remarkably quickly.

## Storing stockings in a Queensland summer

After Christmas, wash or air the stockings, let them dry completely, and store them in a breathable cotton bag in a cool, dry cupboard. Plastic tubs in a hot garage can trap humidity and cause mildew, and heat can soften painted or vinyl lettering.

Whichever method you choose, the name is what makes it. Double-check spellings before you start, especially for that cousin who spells their name differently from everyone else.
