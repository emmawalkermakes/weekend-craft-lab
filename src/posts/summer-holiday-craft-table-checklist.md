The long summer break is a stretch of hot afternoons when it's too warm to be outside and everyone wants something to do. A craft table that's ready to go turns "I'm bored" into an hour of making. One that takes twenty minutes to set up and forty to clean up usually ends up unused.

This is a checklist rather than a project. Work through it once in late November or early December, and the table is ready for the whole holidays. Tick off what you already have, and skip anything that doesn't suit your space.

## The surface

- [ ] **A wipeable cover.** A vinyl or oilcloth tablecloth, or a cheap shower curtain, protects the table from paint, glue and glitter, and lifts off for a shake outside.
- [ ] **A cutting mat** for anything involving a craft knife, so the cover doesn't get sliced.
- [ ] **A stack of scrap paper or newspaper** for the really messy jobs.
- [ ] **Good light.** Near a window is ideal, but out of direct afternoon sun, which bakes paint and glue and makes the table uncomfortably hot.
- [ ] **Airflow.** A fan helps on humid days, but point it away from the table or it'll blow paper and glitter everywhere.

## The basic supplies

You don't need a craft shop's worth of stock. A small, well-chosen kit gets used more than an overflowing one.

- [ ] Plain paper (copy paper is fine) and a few sheets of card
- [ ] Coloured pencils, felt pens and crayons
- [ ] Scissors: one good pair for adults and child-safe pairs if young children will be using the table
- [ ] A ruler and a pencil sharpener
- [ ] Washi tape and clear tape
- [ ] A glue stick and a bottle of PVA glue (see below)
- [ ] Paint: a few colours of washable or acrylic paint, brushes, and a water jar
- [ ] Recycled materials: cardboard tubes, egg cartons, cereal boxes, bottle lids

## Choosing the right glue

Glue is where most craft-table frustration comes from, so it's worth having the right one to hand:

- [ ] **Glue sticks** for paper on paper. Quick, low-mess, and good for younger kids.
- [ ] **PVA (white) glue** for cardboard, collage and heavier paper. It takes longer to dry, especially in humid weather.
- [ ] **Double-sided tape** for neat card-making with no wait.
- [ ] **A low-temperature glue gun**, if you want one, stored separately and used only with an adult nearby. Even low-temp guns can burn, and the nozzle stays hot after it's unplugged.

## Storage that actually gets used

- [ ] **Clear tubs or shoebox-sized containers** so supplies can be seen without opening every lid.
- [ ] **Labels** on each tub: "paper", "drawing", "paint", "sticky things", "recycled".
- [ ] **A jar or mug for pens and pencils**, kept on the table.
- [ ] **A flat folder or a cardboard box for paper** so it doesn't curl or soak up moisture in humid weather.
- [ ] **Lids on paint and glue**, every time. In a Queensland summer, an open PVA bottle forms a skin quickly.

## Somewhere to dry finished work

This is the item that's easiest to forget and makes the biggest difference.

- [ ] **A drying line.** A length of string across a wall or between two chairs, with pegs, takes paintings and paper crafts off the table.
- [ ] **A tray or baking sheet** for 3D pieces that need to stay flat.
- [ ] **A "finished" box** for completed work, so the table doesn't fill up with half-dry projects.

## Safety, briefly

- [ ] Craft knives and glue guns stored up high or in a separate box, out of reach of young children
- [ ] Small items like beads, buttons and googly eyes kept away from toddlers, as they're a choking hazard
- [ ] Paint and glue labels checked for age suitability
- [ ] Glitter used on a tray (or skipped entirely; it's not compulsory)
- [ ] A first-aid kit somewhere you know where to find it

## An idea jar

A blank table can be as off-putting as a blank page. Keep a jar of folded idea slips next to the supplies, and pull one out whenever someone needs a starting point:

- [ ] Make a card for someone you haven't seen in a while
- [ ] Build a town from cardboard boxes
- [ ] Paint the view from the window
- [ ] Make bookmarks for everyone in the house
- [ ] Draw a map of an imaginary island
- [ ] Make paper chains for Christmas

Seasonal projects belong here too. The [paper bag advent calendar](/posts/paper-bag-advent-calendar/) and [potato stamp wrapping paper](/posts/potato-stamp-christmas-wrapping-paper/) both fit a holiday craft table nicely.

## The five-minute pack-up

The secret to a craft table that keeps getting used is that it's quick to reset. Agree on a short routine and stick to it:

- [ ] Lids on paint, glue and pens
- [ ] Brushes rinsed and standing bristles-up to dry
- [ ] Scraps into a "keep" box or the recycling
- [ ] Wet work onto the drying line or tray
- [ ] Tablecloth wiped down or shaken outside
- [ ] Tubs back in their spots

That's it. Set the table up once, keep the supplies simple and the pack-up short, and it'll earn its place through the whole summer break.
