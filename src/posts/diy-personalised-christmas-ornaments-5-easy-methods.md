A personalised ornament is one of the best weekend crafts there is. It's small, it's cheap, you can make a whole batch in an afternoon, and it comes out of the box every December for years. Name ornaments are also lovely gifts for grandparents, teachers and new babies.

Below are five methods, from "ten minutes with a paint pen" to "proper weekend project". Pick one based on the tools you already have.

## Before you start: a few general tips

- **Plan your text.** Short names and years fit best. Write them out on paper at actual size before committing.
- **Practise on scrap.** Paint pens, vinyl and glitter all behave differently on curved surfaces.
- **Work in good light.** Daylight near a window is ideal for checking spacing and spelling.
- **Mind the humidity.** In a sticky Queensland summer, paint and glue take longer to cure. Give everything at least overnight, and longer if it still feels tacky.
- **Ventilate.** Use spray sealers outdoors or in a well-ventilated space, and follow the label.

## Method 1: Paint pen on a plain bauble

**Time:** 30 minutes plus drying
**Skill:** Beginner
**You'll need:** plain baubles (glass or shatterproof plastic), oil-based or acrylic paint pens, rubbing alcohol, a soft cloth, clear acrylic sealer (optional)

1. **Clean the bauble.** Wipe with rubbing alcohol to remove fingerprints and oils so the paint grips.
2. **Mark the centre.** Hang the bauble by its cap and note where the front faces. That's where the name goes.
3. **Write lightly first.** Use a fine-tip pen or pencil-thin first pass, then go over it to thicken the letters.
4. **Add simple details.** Holly leaves, dots, a little star. Keep it simple; less is more on a curve.
5. **Dry and seal.** Let it dry fully, ideally overnight, then add a light coat of clear sealer if you want extra durability.

**Tip:** Rest the bauble in an egg carton or a small cup while you work so it doesn't roll.

## Method 2: Vinyl lettering

**Time:** 45 minutes
**Skill:** Beginner to intermediate
**You'll need:** permanent adhesive vinyl, transfer tape, a cutting machine *or* pre-cut vinyl letters, a scraper or old card

1. **Cut your text.** With a cutting machine, size names to about 3 to 5 cm wide for a standard bauble. Without one, many craft stores sell pre-cut vinyl alphabets.
2. **Weed the design.** Remove the excess vinyl around and inside the letters.
3. **Apply transfer tape.** Burnish it firmly over the letters.
4. **Snip relief cuts.** Make small cuts in the transfer tape between letters so it can curve around the bauble without wrinkling.
5. **Apply from the centre out.** Press the middle letter down first, then work outwards.
6. **Peel slowly.** Pull the transfer tape back at a low angle.

**Tip:** Flat ornaments (timber discs, acrylic stars) are much easier for vinyl than round baubles. Start there if you're new.

## Method 3: Salt dough name ornaments

**Time:** 1 hour active, plus baking and drying
**Skill:** Beginner, great with kids
**You'll need:** 1 cup plain flour, ½ cup salt, about ½ cup water, letter stamps or a toothpick, cookie cutters, a straw, acrylic paint, clear sealer, ribbon

1. **Mix the dough.** Combine flour and salt, then add water a little at a time until it forms a smooth, non-sticky dough.
2. **Roll and cut.** Roll to about 5 mm thick and cut shapes.
3. **Personalise.** Press letter stamps into the dough, write with a toothpick, or press in a child's handprint or fingerprint.
4. **Make the hanging hole.** Use a straw near the top.
5. **Bake low and slow.** Bake on a lined tray at a low oven temperature until hard and dry all the way through. Thicker pieces take longer. Turn them partway through.
6. **Paint and seal.** Once completely cool, paint, then seal *every surface*, including the back and inside the hole.

**Important for Australian summers:** salt dough absorbs moisture from the air. Unsealed ornaments can go soft or grow mould in humid weather. Seal thoroughly, and store them in an airtight container with a silica sachet.

**Safety:** Salt dough isn't food, and the high salt content can be dangerous to pets that chew it. Hang these out of reach of dogs.

## Method 4: Photo ornaments

**Time:** 30 minutes
**Skill:** Beginner
**You'll need:** clear fillable ornaments or flat acrylic photo ornaments, printed photos, scissors or a circle punch, a paint pen for the name and year

1. **Print small.** Print photos at the size of the ornament. A sharp, well-lit photo with the face filling much of the frame works best.
2. **Trim to fit.** For fillable baubles, roll the photo gently and slide it in, then let it unfurl. For flat acrylic ornaments, trim and insert.
3. **Add the name.** Write the name and year on the outside with a paint pen, or on a small tag tied to the hanger.

**Tip:** Glossy photo paper can stick to the inside of plastic in humid weather. Matte paper is more forgiving.

## Method 5: Pyrography or "faux burn" on timber discs

**Time:** 1 to 2 hours
**Skill:** Intermediate
**You'll need:** timber slice ornaments (with bark is lovely), a pyrography pen *or* a fine black permanent marker, pencil, sandpaper, clear matte sealer

1. **Sand lightly.** Smooth the face of the disc with fine sandpaper.
2. **Draw your design in pencil.** Names, a simple tree, a year.
3. **Burn or ink it.** With a pyrography pen, work slowly with light pressure; let the heat do the work. Without one, a fine permanent marker gives a similar look.
4. **Seal.** A matte sealer keeps the natural look and protects against moisture.

**Safety:** Pyrography pens get extremely hot. Use the stand, never leave one on unattended, and keep kids and pets well away. Work in a ventilated space.

## Which method should you choose?

| Method | Cost | Time | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paint pen | Low | Short | Quick batches, adults and older kids |
| Vinyl | Medium | Medium | Crisp lettering, cutting-machine owners |
| Salt dough | Very low | Long (with drying) | Kids, handprints, rustic look |
| Photo ornaments | Low to medium | Short | Grandparents, new babies, pets |
| Timber pyrography | Medium | Long | Rustic, natural decor |

## Storing personalised ornaments

Wrap each one in tissue paper and store in a sturdy box in the coolest, driest part of the house. Garages and roof spaces in Queensland get very hot, which can soften paint, warp plastic and loosen glue. A wardrobe shelf is a better bet.
