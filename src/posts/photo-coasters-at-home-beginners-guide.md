Photo coasters are the perfect weekend craft. They use cheap materials, make a genuinely useful gift, and are forgiving enough for a first project. A set of four with family photos, pets or holiday snaps is a great Christmas present for grandparents, and they earn their keep on humid summer nights when cold drinks drip everywhere.

This guide covers three methods, from simplest to most durable, plus the bits that most tutorials skip: choosing the right photos, sealing properly, and dealing with heat and condensation.

## What you'll need (basic method)

- Plain ceramic tiles, around 10 × 10 cm (hardware stores often sell them individually) **or** cork coaster blanks
- Photos printed on regular laser or inkjet paper (not glossy photo paper for this method)
- Decoupage glue and sealer (for example, a Mod Podge-type product)
- A soft flat brush or foam brush
- Scissors or a craft knife and cutting mat
- Clear acrylic spray sealer suitable for the surface
- Cork or felt sheets for backing, plus strong craft glue
- Optional: fine sandpaper, a ruler, a brayer or old card for smoothing

## Step 1: Choose and prepare your photos

Photo choice makes or breaks this project.

- **Pick sharp photos.** Blurry shots look worse when printed.
- **Fill the frame.** Faces or the main subject should take up a decent portion of the square. Tiny people in a big landscape disappear on a 10 cm tile.
- **Crop square** in your phone's photo editor before printing.
- **Print slightly smaller than the tile**, leaving a few millimetres of border, or exactly the tile size if you want edge-to-edge.

If you're using an inkjet printer, the ink can smear when it gets wet with glue. Two fixes: print on a laser printer (or at a print shop), or let inkjet prints dry for a few hours and then spray them lightly with clear acrylic sealer before gluing.

## Step 2: Clean the tiles

Wipe each tile with a damp cloth, then with rubbing alcohol, and let it dry. Dust and oils are the main cause of bubbles and peeling.

## Step 3: Glue the photo down

1. Brush a thin, even layer of decoupage glue onto the tile.
2. Place the photo face up, starting from one edge.
3. Smooth it out from the centre to the edges with a brayer or old card to push out air bubbles.
4. Wipe away excess glue around the edges.
5. Let it dry for at least 20 to 30 minutes, longer on humid days.

## Step 4: Seal the top

1. Brush a thin coat of decoupage medium over the whole surface, including the edges of the photo.
2. Let it dry fully, then add another coat. Brush in a different direction each time for an even finish.
3. Aim for three or four thin coats rather than one thick one. Thick coats stay tacky.
4. Leave overnight.

## Step 5: Add a protective spray

Outdoors or in a well-ventilated space, apply a light coat of clear acrylic sealer. Follow the product's instructions on recoat times. This helps with moisture resistance and stops the surface feeling tacky in humid weather.

**Important:** most decoupage finishes are water-*resistant*, not waterproof, and not heat-proof. These coasters are for cold drinks and the odd cup of tea, not for hot pots straight off the stove. Wipe them clean; don't soak them.

## Step 6: Add the backing

Cut cork or felt squares slightly smaller than the tile. Glue them to the back with strong craft glue and press under a stack of books overnight. The backing stops the tile scratching furniture.

## Method 2: Photo transfer (the "vintage" look)

This gives a softer, slightly faded look where the photo becomes part of the tile surface.

1. Print your photo **mirrored** (flipped horizontally) on a laser printer. Inkjet prints usually don't transfer well.
2. Apply a thick layer of photo transfer medium to the printed side.
3. Press it face down onto the tile and smooth out the bubbles.
4. Leave it to dry fully; check the product's instructions, but overnight is usual.
5. Dampen the paper and gently rub it away with your fingertips, revealing the image.
6. Let it dry, then seal as above.

Expect some imperfections. That's part of the charm, but it's not the method to choose if you want crisp detail.

## Method 3: Resin topcoat (most durable)

A clear epoxy resin topcoat makes coasters glossy and more water-resistant. It's also the method that needs the most care.

1. Glue and seal the photo with decoupage medium first, so the resin doesn't soak into the paper and darken it.
2. Mix a coating resin exactly as the instructions say. Measure carefully.
3. Pour a thin layer over the tile, spread it to the edges and let it self-level.
4. Pop bubbles by gently passing a heat gun or a long lighter over the surface (follow the product's guidance).
5. Cover to keep dust off and let it cure for the full time listed on the product.

**Safety and climate notes:**

- Wear nitrile gloves and work in a ventilated space. Resin can irritate skin and some people develop sensitivities over time.
- In Brisbane summer heat, resin cures faster and gives you less working time. Mix smaller batches.
- High humidity can cause cloudiness on some resins. Avoid pouring on a very humid day if you can.
- Even cured resin can soften or mark with hot mugs. Treat these as cold-drink coasters.

## Personalisation ideas

- **Family set:** one photo per person, with their name in small text along the bottom.
- **Pet set:** four photos of the dog in different moods.
- **Holiday set:** four favourite photos from a family trip, with the place name and year.
- **Kids' art set:** scan children's drawings instead of photos. Great for grandparents.
- **Wedding or anniversary set:** use photos from the day and add the date.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Bubbles under the photo | Too little glue, or not smoothed | Smooth from the centre out; prick tiny bubbles with a pin and press flat |
| Ink smudging | Inkjet print got wet | Laser print, or spray-seal inkjet prints first |
| Surface feels tacky | Coats too thick, or humidity | Thinner coats, longer drying, finish with acrylic spray |
| Cloudy resin | Humidity or moisture | Pour on a drier day; ensure the base is fully dry |
| Edges lifting | Not sealed over the edges | Brush sealer over the photo edges each coat |

## Packaging them as a gift

Stack four coasters with squares of baking paper between them, tie with twine, and add a small tag with care instructions: "Wipe clean. Not for hot pots." That little note will save someone from ruining them in the dishwasher.

Photo coasters are a forgiving first project, and once you've made one set you'll start seeing tiles everywhere. Happy making.
